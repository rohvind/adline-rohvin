Booking tips

  • Reservations open 60 days ahead, so seats for 30 and 31 January 2027 go on sale around the first week of December 2026. Book early.
  • Mangalore has two stations: Mangaluru Central (MAQ) in the city, and Mangaluru Junction (MAJN) about 10 km south. Both are 15 to 30 minutes from the venue.
  • Timings are from the current timetable. Confirm on IRCTC or the NTES app before you travel.
  • From Mysuru, the Kannur Express (16517) picks up around 11:30 PM and gets you to Mangalore by morning.

Flight tips

  • Mangalore International Airport (IXE) is in Bajpe, about 35 minutes from AJ Grand Elite by cab.
  • For the Sunday 11 AM celebration, land in Mangalore by 9:30 AM. The 6:00 AM Bangalore flight gets you there with time to spare.
  • Prepaid taxis and Ola/Uber are available at the airport. Rough fare: ₹700 to ₹900 to the city.

Driving tips

  • Honestly, the best plan is to travel a day early. Reach Mangalore on Saturday, give yourself a buffer, and start the celebration without racing the clock.
  • If you're driving on the day itself: start by 3 AM from Bengaluru, or 4 to 5 AM from Mysuru, to reach AJ Grand Elite well before the 11 AM start.
  • The Shiradi Ghat on the Bengaluru and Hassan routes has tight curves. Drive it in daylight and keep to the left on blind bends.
  • The Madikeri route is the most scenic but adds an hour. Worth it if you've never driven through Coorg. Coffee stop at Madikeri.
  • Fuel up at Hassan or Sakleshpur. Stretches between them have fewer pumps.
  • Carry motion sickness tablets if anyone's prone to it. The ghat sections are winding.

A little Mangalore primer

If you're flying in, stay a day longer. Mangalore is a coastal town with a 400-year Catholic story, a serious food reputation, and some of the quieter beaches on the west coast.

Where to eat

Mangalore takes its seafood seriously. These are the names worth planning a meal around.

Giri Manja's

Mangalore's most famous fish fry, in a tiny room near Mannagudda. Go early, be prepared to wait, order the anjal fry.

Mannagudda · must-try

Machali

Fine-dining seafood in Falnir. Fresh catch, Mangalorean preparations, and an actual sit-down menu. Great for a nice evening out.

Falnir · dinner-worthy

Shetty Lunch Home

The Mangalore institution for Kori Rotti, Neer Dosa, and Chicken Ghee Roast. Lunch-only crowd favourite.

Kandathil · lunch only

Pabba's Ideal Café

Home of the legendary Gadbad ice cream. The city's sweet full-stop after any Mangalorean meal.

Lalbagh · dessert stop

Janatha Deluxe

Pure vegetarian stalwart in the heart of the city. South Indian thalis, dosas, filter coffee, all done properly. The reliable pick if you're skipping the seafood trail.

Hampankatta · pure veg

Churches to see

Three of Mangalore's heritage parishes, each with its own story.

Milagres Church

Our Lady of Miracles. Over 300 years old, right in the heart of the city. One of the oldest surviving Catholic churches on the west coast.

Hampankatta · central

St Vincent Ferrer Church

Valencia. A beloved parish church with a strong Mangalorean Catholic community and quiet, well-kept grounds. Worth a stop if you're in the area.

Valencia · parish church

Guardian Angels Church

Angelore. A heritage Mangalorean Catholic church with deep roots in the community. Quieter than the city ones, and worth the short drive out.

Angelore · heritage

St. Lawrence Shrine Basilica

Attur, Karkala. A minor basilica with over two centuries of history, long considered one of coastal Karnataka's most significant Catholic pilgrimage sites. Worth the drive for the setting alone.

Attur, Karkala · 58 km · pilgrimage

Infant Jesus Shrine

Bikarnakatte. A popular city shrine dedicated to the Infant Jesus of Prague. Thursday novenas draw crowds from across the region, and the setting is quietly beautiful on any other day.

Bikarnakatte · shrine
